The one negative coefficient

A positivity criterion for graviton scattering worked up to knife order 8 and then failed. It fails by two parts in ten thousand — and all of those parts sit in a single coefficient. That is enough to repair it.

Where the failure lives

Expand the object at the shore and collect powers of the distance below it. Every coefficient is a sum with all-positive parts except one: the coefficient of yJ−2. Measured, at 7 depths:

The repair, and why it is a proof

Group the negative term with its two neighbours:

cJ-3 yJ-3 + cJ-2 yJ-2 + cJ-1 yJ-1 = yJ-3 ( cJ-3 + cJ-2 y + cJ-1 y2 )

The outer coefficients are positive, so this block is nonnegative for every y exactly when the discriminant is:

(R) 4 cJ-1 cJ-3 − cJ-22 ≥ 0

Built exactly over the field of the region and expanded, (R) has no negative monomial at all for every J from 7 to 29, and it stays certified all the way to J = 50 once the region is read correctly. Every variable of the region is nonnegative there, so all-nonnegative monomials is not evidence — it is a proof.

What breaks, and how narrowly

At J = 30 the certificate stops on the full region, with 39 negative monomials out of 6152. They are not scattered: every one carries the same thL3, y0, K31, differing only in the remaining variable — one line in the exponent lattice. And that break is an artefact of where it was tested. The structure above holds only for n ≥ 2J−3, which at J = 30 means n ≥ 57; the region as parametrised starts lower, so part of what was being certified lies outside the regime the proof serves. Restricted to its own regime, the same build gives 6152 monomials and zero negatives. The inequality did not break there; the test domain did. It does break further on: with the matching restriction the build gives 70 negative monomials at J = 31 and 392 at J = 40, so manifest positivity is a tool with a range. Past it one Bernstein change of basis along thL -- which lives on [0,1] in this region, not on the whole ray -- certifies again: zero negative coefficients at every depth tested up to J = 50.

What this does and does not settle

Proved: the repair inequality, on the whole region, at those depths — and with it, positivity at the depths where the criterion alone had failed. Not proved: that the remaining coefficients are nonnegative, which is still a measurement and holds only while n ≥ 2J−3. One leg on a certificate, one on a well-tested measurement.
